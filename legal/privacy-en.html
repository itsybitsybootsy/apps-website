No data protection officer

Q Systems has not appointed a data protection officer. Art. 37 (1) GDPR requires an appointment only in three cases: for public authorities and bodies (lit. a), where the core activities consist of large-scale regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects (lit. b), or where the core activities consist of large-scale processing of special categories of data under Art. 9 GDPR or of data relating to criminal convictions under Art. 10 GDPR. In Austria, § 5 DSG additionally regulates the position, duty of confidentiality and right to refuse to give evidence of the data protection officer, but does not create any obligation to appoint one beyond Art. 37 GDPR.

In our assessment, none of the three cases applies:

  • lit. a: Q Systems is not a public authority or public body.
  • lit. b: We do not monitor anyone. There is no tracking, no analytics SDKs, no profiling and no user accounts; the pseudonymous device identifier serves solely security and abuse prevention.
  • lit. c: Skinmetrics processes health data within the meaning of Art. 9 (1) GDPR. However, this processing takes place on the user's device and — where an AI analysis is requested — at the service providers named in chapters 5 and 6. Q Systems itself neither stores this content centrally nor permanently; the relay service only passes it through. Measured against the criteria in Recital 91 GDPR (number of data subjects, volume of data, duration and geographic scope of the processing by the controller), we currently do not assess the processing as "large-scale".

This is our own assessment, not a finding by the data protection authority. We will review it again as soon as user numbers increase significantly or we should begin processing data server-side. If you think this assessment is wrong, write to us — we will engage with it.

5 · Skinmetrics

The following chapter describes what data Skinmetrics processes, where it is stored and what leaves the device. There is no user account and no user database containing your content on our side.

5.1 · Skinmetrics

Skinmetrics processes sensitive skin data — which is why this is the section you should really read. The short version up front: For every AI analysis, your skin photo and selected profile details leave your iPhone. They go via a relay service of Q Systems at Cloudflare to OpenRouter and from there to hosted open AI models (Meta Llama 4 and Alibaba Qwen3-VL). The app does not send directly to an AI provider and contains no access key of an AI provider. We explain everything else below.

What data Skinmetrics processes

Skinmetrics has no user account and no login. We do not keep user profiles with content on a server. The bulk of what the app collects arises and stays on your device:

  • Images of your skin and face — from Quick Scan and Full Scan, from the daily check-in and from other capture functions of the app.
  • Health-related details from your profile, partly as free text that you write yourself: skin type, age range, details on skin cancer history, sun exposure and chronic skin conditions, cosmetic allergies (including details), pregnancy and breastfeeding, skin concerns and skin goals, and the AI assessment as text.
  • History and usage data of the app: your self-rating, mood, free notes, skin score, spot tracking, streaks, your AM/PM routine and the products you have chosen.
  • A pseudonymous device identifier for our relay service (Apple App Attest, see below).
  • No location. The app merely stores a rough region code of your own choosing such as "EU" or "US" so that the seasonal logic is correct. This is not location tracking.
  • No inference of gender or age from the face. The app estimates neither your gender nor your age from your photos.

The details on conditions, allergies, pregnancy and skin cancer history are health data. Together with a photo of your skin, they are special categories of personal data Art. 9 (1) GDPR. A basic prohibition on processing applies to such data; we may process it only if one of the narrowly interpreted exceptions of Art. 9 (2) GDPR applies (cf. CJEU, C-252/21). In our case, that is your explicit consent.

Where your data is stored: on your iPhone

  • Storage is local in SwiftData. Photos are stored alongside as .externalStorage attachments, not in the database file.
  • The app applies FileProtectionType.complete to the data store's directory. While your iPhone is locked, the files are encrypted by the system and unreadable.
  • No iCloud sync, no CloudKit. We do not synchronize anything between your devices.
  • The App Group exists only so that the widgets can display something. It holds a small snapshot with skin score, streak and routine progress — no photos and no health texts. This is a deliberate design decision: widget content may be visible on the lock screen.
  • No advertising, tracking or crash SDKs. No Firebase, no Sentry, no Crashlytics, no Mixpanel, no PostHog, no Amplitude, no RevenueCat, no ad SDK. No App Tracking Transparency, because nothing is tracked.
  • Optional anonymous product statistics: An optional, anonymous product statistics feature (provider Supabase) is prepared in the app's code. At launch it is switched off and not configured; no data is transmitted through it. If it were ever activated, it would be opt-in only (off by default), would transmit only counters and labels — no photos and no health values — and would use a random identifier generated per installation. We would update this policy before activating it.

Backup — you need to know this. Because your data is stored in the normal app container, depending on your Apple settings it is included in the iPhone backup: that is, in the iCloud backup or in the local backup on your Mac/PC. There, Apple's rules and encryption apply, not ours. We have no influence over this. If you do not want this, switch off the backup for Skinmetrics in the iOS settings under Apple ID → iCloud → Backup → Apps or use an encrypted local backup.

What leaves your device during an AI analysis

For every AI analysis, the app sends an encrypted (HTTPS) request to the relay service skinmetrics-ai. This is a Cloudflare Worker operated by Q Systems in Q Systems' own Cloudflare account. The service first checks that the request comes from a genuine installation of the app (Apple App Attest) and then forwards it to OpenRouter (OpenRouter, Inc., USA). OpenRouter relays to hosted open models: Meta Llama 4 Scout/Maverick and Alibaba Qwen3-VL. Google or Gemini is not used in the published app; the app contains no access key of an AI provider.

It is not only the image that is transmitted. Per request, the following is sent:

  • the photo or photos, previously reduced to a maximum edge length of 1024 pixels and compressed as JPEG;
  • the profile details relevant to the suitability of products and routines: age range, skin type, skin concerns and goals and — only if you have provided them — details on pregnancy/breastfeeding and on allergies (including details);
  • the names of the products in your routine or the products used;
  • a summary of your history (e.g. number of check-ins, average self-rating and trend).

In plain terms: health data under Art. 9 GDPR leaves your device during an AI analysis and is transmitted to US companies.

Never sent to the AI are your answers on skin cancer history and on chronic conditions. They remain exclusively on your device. Your saved photos from the history, mood entries and free notes are not part of these requests.

What our relay service stores — and what it does not

  • Not stored and not logged: Images, prompts and responses are neither stored nor logged by the service (the service's observability and logging features are switched off).
  • Pseudonymous device identifier: Per installation, the service stores the identifier of the App Attest key, the associated public key and a counter. This is a pseudonymous device identifier with which we cannot identify you by name. The entry is automatically deleted after 180 days without use.
  • Quota counters (abuse and cost limiting) expire within one day at the latest.
  • IP addresses are not stored by us in plain text. For rate limiting, we use only a hash value (HMAC) formed with a daily rotating salt. For technical reasons, Cloudflare as infrastructure provider sees the IP address on every connection.

What OpenRouter and the model providers do

With every request, we instruct OpenRouter to use only providers that do not store inputs and do not use them for training (provider routing with data_collection=deny and zero data retention, zdr). If no provider meeting this condition is found, the service aborts (it "fails closed") instead of sending the request to another provider.

What we cannot guarantee. The models are operated by third parties selected by OpenRouter; we do not know these subcontractors in advance in individual cases. We have no direct influence over their processing and rely on them keeping their commitments. We do not promise that "nothing is stored" at third parties; we promise that we only query providers that commit to non-storage and non-use for training. On OpenRouter's own logging: [[ check OpenRouter's statements on its own logging of metadata and add here ]].

Technically, the app uses a URLSessionConfiguration.ephemeral (no persistent caches, cookies or credentials on the device) and enforces TLS from version 1.2.

AI results can be wrong

The analyses are AI estimates and can be inaccurate. Lighting conditions, image quality and skin tone in particular can distort the results; models may perform worse for certain skin tones. If you have concerns, do not rely on the app but seek medical advice.

On what legal basis we do this

For special categories, in addition to a legal basis under Art. 6, an exception under Art. 9 is required.

  • Operation of the app — history, routine, streaks, widgets, subscription management: Art. 6 (1) lit. b GDPR (performance of the usage contract).
  • Pseudonymous device identifier, quota and abuse protection in the relay service: Art. 6 (1) lit. b GDPR (performance of a contract) or Art. 6 (1) lit. f GDPR (legitimate interest in security and abuse prevention).
  • Skin photos and health details, in particular their transmission for the AI analysis: Art. 9 (2) lit. a GDPR in conjunction with Art. 6 (1) lit. a GDPR — your explicit consent.
  • Local notifications: your consent via the iOS system dialog, which can be withdrawn at any time in the iOS settings.

The app obtains the consent for photos and health data separately and explicitly within the app — not as part of general terms and not through a mere "Continue". It is versioned: if the processing changes, we ask again. You can withdraw it at any time, as easily as you gave it, in the app under Settings → Privacy Art. 7 (3) GDPR. The withdrawal takes effect for the future and does not affect the lawfulness of the processing carried out until then. The burden of proof for valid consent lies with us Art. 7 (1) GDPR.

Consent and voluntariness

Consent is required for the AI functions: without it, the app's AI analysis is not available, because the analysis cannot be provided without transmitting the photo. The consent is standalone and not coupled to agreement to other processing; there is no advertising or tracking processing to which you would additionally have to consent. Transfer to the USA

OpenRouter, Cloudflare and the model providers selected by OpenRouter may have their seat or servers in the United States. Each analysis can therefore be a third-country transfer under Chapter V GDPR. The order of examination: first an adequacy decision Art. 45 GDPR (EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, where the recipient is certified), then appropriate safeguards Art. 46 GDPR (standard contractual clauses), and only last the derogations of Art. 49 GDPR.

  • Art. 45 — Data Privacy Framework. Certification status of the recipients: OpenRouter [[ verify DPF status ]], Cloudflare [[ verify DPF status ]], model providers [[ verify DPF status per provider ]].

On the status of the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (2026)

Several of our recipients are based in the USA. For transfers there, the European Commission's adequacy decision of 10 July 2023 on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (DPF), Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795, applies. It permits transfers to US companies that have self-certified under the DPF with the US Department of Commerce, without additional safeguards under Art. 46 GDPR.

The current status, checked on [Date of last check]:

  • The General Court of the European Union (EGC), by judgment of 3 September 2025 in Case T-553/23 (Latombe v Commission), dismissed the action for annulment against the adequacy decision and confirmed its validity.
  • An appeal to the Court of Justice was lodged against this judgment in October 2025. The proceedings are still pending.
  • The adequacy decision has neither been annulled nor suspended and remains a valid basis for transfers until a decision to the contrary.
  • It remains critical that the Privacy and Civil Liberties Oversight Board (PCLOB) has lacked a quorum since the removal of several members in January 2025. This weakens one of the pillars of the framework — independent oversight of US intelligence services and support for the redress mechanism.

Honestly: it cannot be ruled out that the CJEU will later strike down the adequacy decision, just as it did Safe Harbor and Privacy Shield before. 9 · Data security

Under Art. 32 GDPR, we must take technical and organizational measures appropriate to the risk. We describe here what the apps actually do — no measures that we have not implemented.

What is implemented

  • iOS app sandbox: Each app runs in its own directory, isolated by the system. Other apps cannot access its data.
  • Encryption at rest: iOS encrypts the device storage. For the directory of the local database, Skinmetrics additionally sets the strongest iOS protection class (Complete File Protection). The data can thus be decrypted only while the device is unlocked. A prerequisite is that you have set up a device passcode on your iPhone — without a passcode, there is no effective protection at rest.
  • Transport encryption: All network connections run over HTTPS with TLS 1.2 or higher. Unencrypted connections are not permitted in the apps.
  • No persistent network caches: For the connection to the relay service, Skinmetrics uses an ephemeral session configuration, so that responses are not cached on disk.
  • Data minimization before transmission: Images are reduced to a maximum edge length of 1024 pixels and compressed before sending. Only what is needed for the respective analysis is transmitted.
  • No accounts, no passwords: Since there are no user accounts, there are also no credentials that could be stolen and no central database containing your content that could be compromised. Server-side there is only the pseudonymous App Attest identifier.
  • Local processing where possible: Face detection and image alignment in Skinmetrics run exclusively on the device via Apple's Vision framework.

What we expressly do not guarantee

No system is absolutely secure. In particular:

Transmission over the internet. The transmission of data over the internet may have security vulnerabilities. Seamless protection against access by third parties is not possible.

Processing at service providers. As soon as content leaves your app, the security organization of Cloudflare, OpenRouter and the model providers selected by OpenRouter applies. We cannot make any commitments in this regard that go beyond their statements.

Relay service and device verification. Requests run through the relay service of Q Systems (Cloudflare Worker). It verifies each installation with Apple App Attest, limits requests per device and stores no images, prompts or responses. No system is free of errors; App Attest requires a genuine device with current iOS and is not an absolute guarantee against abuse.

Exported data. If you create and share a data export in Skinmetrics, this file leaves the protected area of the app. What happens to it afterwards is up to you and the destination to which you send it.

Your device. Without a device passcode, with a rooted or jailbroken device, or if the unlocked device is handed to third parties, the protection mechanisms described do not apply.

Reporting security vulnerabilities

If you find a security vulnerability, please report it to privacy@q-systems.app. We review every report. In the event of a personal data breach posing a risk to you, we will inform the data protection authority under Art. 33 GDPR and, in the case of high risk, the data subjects under Art. 34 GDPR. Since we have no contact details of our users, notification in this case takes place via a public notice on this website and, where possible, in the app.
